:END:

* Motivation and agreed conceptual distinction
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: motivation-and-agreed-conceptual-distinction
:END:
Inspired by the user's experience of the 4ms SMR pitch-CV mode, especially
circulating pitches, variable spacing and slow slew between positions. SMR
behaviour was recalled from memory, not verified against its documentation.
This is not intended as an exact emulation.

Rotation distance and voice spacing are independent. Moving one position need
not give an output the pitch previously assigned to its neighbour. A circular
pitch list and persistent voice identities are central ideas. Existing
ordinary arp and arp-to-chord behaviour should remain unchanged.

* Proposed pitch behaviour
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: proposed-pitch-behaviour
:END:
- Four voices, matching CARBON's four CV/gate pairs; MIDI chord output too.
- Initial wheel size: 24 positions, containing successive notes of the
  effective scale, starting at the input note quantized using effective Root.
- Chromatic gives 24 successive semitones; other scales span a wider range.
- For voice numbers 0, 1, 2, 3:

#+begin_src text
position = (Rotation + voice_number * Spread) modulo 24

Spread 3, Rotation 0:   0   3   6   9
Spread 3, Rotation 1:   1   4   7  10
Spread 3, Rotation 16: 16  19  22   1
#+end_src

- Wrap-around intentionally moves a voice back to a low pitch. Do not sort
  voices by pitch or reassign their outputs when they cross.
- Omit pitches outside MIDI/CV capabilities; no clamping or second wrap.
- Some spreads repeat positions. Preserve four logical voices; duplicate MIDI
  pitches need safe note ownership/release handling.

* Proposed sequencer behaviour
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: proposed-sequencer-behaviour
:END:
- Separate Arp Speed choice, Wheel, after Chord; Arp must be enabled.
- First eligible positive-velocity Note anchors the wheel and gives velocity.
  Additional Notes on that step are ignored in this initial proposal.
- Hrm supplies effective scale/root. Transpose and Bias apply exactly once;
  precise ordering needs design and tests. Inv/Compact initially excluded.
- Rotation and Spread retain their values between steps.
- Empty or gate-blocked steps create no attack; existing tails can expire.
- Gate duration follows Chord: actual step interval times Track Gate. Stored
  input lengths/delays/ratchets and Arp Gate are ignored in Wheel.
- New sequence attacks update the anchor and retrigger the group. Overlapping
  groups reuse the four voices and glide from their current pitches.
- No automatic latch or indefinite sustain in the initial proposal.
- Stop, mute, disabling Arp and leaving Wheel release its voices.

* Proposed controls
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: proposed-controls
:END:
| Parameter | Initial range                     | Meaning                                    |
|-----------+-----------------------------------+--------------------------------------------|
| Rotation  | 0--23, wrapping                   | Move all positions around the wheel        |
| Spread    | 1--12                             | Distance between voices in wheel positions |
| Slew      | Off plus existing glide durations | Time to a new pitch target                 |

Suggested defaults: Rotation 0, Spread 2, Slew Off. A major-scale wheel then
starts with a four-note stack of thirds.

Tentative contextual panel mapping while Wheel is selected, outside Step Edit:

- Arp Type encoder edits Rotation instead of arp type.
- Arp Gate encoder edits Spread instead of arp gate.
- Arp menu exposes Rotation, Spread and Slew explicitly.
- Example readout: =Wheel Rot:5 Spr:3=; slew appears in the menu.
- Leaving Wheel restores ordinary control meanings, retaining previous arp
  type/gate settings. Mapping availability and UI fit need verification.

While a group is active, Rotation/Spread edits retarget pitches immediately,
without extending gate duration or retriggering gates. Silent edits prepare
only the next attack. Slew edits affect subsequent retargeting. Use the
existing curve setting initially rather than adding another Wheel parameter;
its exact source and persistence need clarification.

* CV slew and MIDI limitations
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: cv-slew-and-midi-limitations
:END:
CV is the primary target for continuous chord morphing:

- Reserve a four-voice CV group for one Wheel track.
- Fixed voice-to-output assignment and independent slew for all four voices.
- Mid-glide retargeting starts from the actual current pitch.
- At wrap-around, glide from high to low through the pitch range. The target
  selection wraps, not the voltage trajectory.
- Gate handling is independent of pitch movement.

Existing CV glide is groundwork, not an already implemented four-voice
solution: it currently targets mono pitch groups. Stable polyphonic voice
assignment, resource ownership and independent slew need investigation and
testing.

Ordinary MIDI on one channel cannot provide four independent continuous pitch
glides. Initially retarget using correctly paired Note Off/On messages; do not
promise SMR-like MIDI morphing. Six independent pitch CV outputs would require
additional hardware, such as an external MIDI-to-CV interface.

* Persistence and possible later work
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: persistence-and-possible-later-work
:END:
Proposed eventual settings: Rotation, Spread and Slew per track/scene. Review
storage, migration, snapshot/undo and host-tool compatibility before choosing
an encoding. Dedicated CCi/Remote automation could follow panel validation.

Possible staged prototype, only after renewed approval:

1. Establish wheel calculation, pitch boundaries and panel controls.
2. Verify stable four-output CV assignment and resource ownership.
3. Implement/test independent slew and deliberate gate/retrigger semantics.
4. Check hardware musical behaviour and realtime cost before release claims.

* Separate future idea: track magnetism (DEFERRED)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: separate-future-idea-track-magnetism-deferred
:END:
Record only; no implementation requested. Independent of Wheel mode.

- Bias-like, directed pitch influences: each target track can select up to two
  source tracks, each with Attract/Repel direction, probability and strength.
  Mutual attraction requires configuring both directions.
- Remembered evolution: small pitch changes accumulate per pattern position in
  a non-destructive playback layer; stored notes remain unchanged.
- Original notes always attract: each position's original pitch is an implicit
  home attractor. Removing/muting an external source removes its influence,
  not the accumulated displacement; home attraction gradually restores the
  phrase when no other external influence counteracts it.
- Example: A and B attract each other; activating strongly repulsive C
  disturbs them. Muting C lets them recover under remaining attraction/home. A
  shared repeller can move both in the same direction, not necessarily
  separate them. Home attraction can also prevent complete convergence.
- Candidate calculation: sample source references from a common pre-update
  state and combine home plus external tendencies before choosing a bounded
  movement. Avoid track-processing-order dependence. Prefer small scale-degree
  changes; do not retune already sounding notes.
- Possible controls: two source slots, direction/probability/strength per
  slot, home strength, evolution rate and displacement limit. Lock freezes all
  evolution including home return; Reset restores the original immediately.
- Preserve ordinary Bias transposition/control routing; a second magnetic
  source does not imply extending CCi/Hrm/Inv routing.

Open: strength as movement size versus directional weight/probability; update
cadence; source pitch reference and rests/polyphony; equal-pitch escape rules;
scale/root/transpose ordering; exact home-return rule; scene/edit/persistence
and snapshot/undo semantics. These are discussion proposals, not a settled
specification. Resume only on explicit user request.

* Separate future idea: early notes / negative micro-timing (DEFERRED)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: separate-future-idea-early-notes-deferred
:END:
Status: DEFERRED (2026-11-23). Record only; do not implement until the user
explicitly resumes it. Reason: first confirm stability of the existing
feature set; Rec Timing Fine (=jwk-rt2=) already gives sub-step
recording resolution, which covers most of the need. Role model: Elektron
micro-timing. Rule kept: no note ever starts before the step that holds it.

** Concept
- Storage: a note that belongs to home step /h/ and plays /E/ ticks early is
  stored in a free slot of step /h-1/ with an "early" delay code. Delay codes
  0-103 and 255 are in use; 104-254 are free. No layout change (20480 bytes).
- Playback: on /h-1/ the early note starts at step length minus /E/, i.e. an
  ordinary positive delay. Engine change is small.
- Display and editing: Step Edit, tracker and lanes show the note on its home
  step /h/ with a negative offset (=-1/8=, =-6t=); /h-1/ hides it. In Step
  Edit, Dly below 0 on /h/ moves the note to a free slot of /h-1/; turning
  back past 0 moves it home.
- Recording: with Rec Timing Fine, an attack in the last half of step
  /h-1/ is stored as /h/ early, not as /h-1/ with a large delay.

** Decisions taken (user, 2026-11-23)
1. Range: up to half a step early - fractions -1/8, -1/4, -1/3, -1/2, or
   ticks -1..-48.
2. Conditions (Prb/Euc/Pre/!Pr/Scn) and Pattern/Euclid masks: the early note
   follows the step that stores it (/h-1/); Step Edit warns when the home
   step has a condition. Documented as a limitation.
3. Forward direction only: early notes play only when the track runs forward
   (not reverse/pendulum/random).
4. Delivery in three candidates: (1) engine, format and recording;
   (2) display; (3) Step Edit editing. After (1) early notes are usable via
   recording and carbon-backup.

** Points to handle
- Exclude early notes from voicing (Inv), bias note choice, ratchet and Gli
  on the storing step /h-1/.
- Region start: events are shared across scenes, regions are not; "previous"
  means previous step number. If /h/ is the region's first step, /h-1/ is
  outside the region: refuse in Step Edit ("no room"); recording falls back
  to the current Fine behaviour (region end).
- No free slot on /h-1/ (six events): refuse in Step Edit; recording keeps
  the note on /h/.
- Compatibility: older firmware rejects the new delay codes, so a song
  version bump (as for Scn in 1.37); carbon-backup needs a JSON delay kind,
  e.g. ={"kind":"early","ticks":6}=.

** Effort estimate
| Part                                         | Size         |
|----------------------------------------------+--------------|
| Engine: early codes, exclusions              | small-medium |
| Song check, version bump, carbon-backup JSON | small-medium |
| Recording (Fine produces early notes)         | small        |
| Display: tracker, lanes, compact/expanded    | medium       |
| Step Edit: negative Dly that moves notes     | medium-large |
| Tests                                        | medium       |

* Separate future idea: remote song editing, LLM "live coding", WoT (DEFERRED)

Status: DEFERRED (2026-11-23). Record only; exploratory. Do not implement
until the user explicitly resumes it.

** Goal
- A computer edits individual parts of the song in RAM while CARBON plays:
  no special mode, no stopped transport (unlike the flash transfer).
- Use 1: an LLM with a CARBON tool for natural-language "live coding",
  possibly by voice: CARBON as a backing track that takes spoken commands.
- Use 2: a test of the W3C Web of Things standard: a CARBON Thing
  Description with commands as WoT affordances.

** Current state (assessed)
- carbon-backup SysEx (0x70-0x73) reads/writes flash sectors: stopped
  transport, five non-atomic 4 KB commits per song, then LOAD on the panel.
- MIDI remote control (midi_ctrl.c) triggers scenes and live CCi parameters
  while playing; no song edits, no readback.
- The playing song is a RAM structure; Step Edit already changes it while
  playing via song_set_*(); the engine reads it every step. New SysEx
  commands run in the panel task could call the same setters.

** Proposed architecture
LLM / voice -> host gateway (HTTP, WoT TD, optional MCP) -> SysEx over USB
MIDI -> CARBON. Firmware stays small and general; musical logic lives in the
gateway (which can grow out of carbon-backup: song model, JSON, SysEx).

Firmware primitives:
- Read: step events, track/scene/song settings, whole song RAM in chunks.
- Write: set event, clear step, set parameter; same validation as the loader.
- Batches applied at the next bar line (no half-applied loop); one undo point
  per batch.
- Notifications: stepEdited, sceneChanged, transport, playhead (rate-limited).
- Save via command or panel.
Rules: refuse writes while loading/saving (BUSY); recording: refuse or allow
only non-recorded tracks; Step Edit on the same step refreshes; last write
wins.

** LLM points
- The model must see the song as text (tracker-style summaries, as in
  carbon-backup view) and act on the real state.
- High-level musical actions ("chorus chords step-wise", "fill in bar 4",
  "thin hats in the verse") are gateway actions on firmware primitives.
- Dry run as tracker text for large changes; "undo that" = Undo.
- Latency dominated by the LLM; bar-quantised application hides it.

** WoT points
- Properties: tempo, scene, track settings, Rec Timing. Actions:
  setStepEvent, clearStep, applyBatch, loadSong, saveSong, undo. Events:
  stepEdited, sceneChanged, playheadMoved, transport.
- No MIDI protocol binding exists: gateway exposes HTTP and maps to SysEx,
  or define an experimental SysEx binding (own URI scheme).
- carbon-backup event JSON can serve as the setStepEvent data schema.
- LLM tool definitions can be generated from the TD: one description for
  people, WoT clients and the LLM.
- The TD describes the gateway interface; the firmware has no network stack.

** Suggested order
1. Firmware read commands + notifications (safe while playing).
2. Gateway: live song mirror, text summaries, TD over HTTP.
3. Firmware writes: bar-quantised batches, undo.
4. Gateway: LLM tools from the TD, a few high-level musical actions; voice.
Steps 1-2 are useful without an LLM (live mirror on a big screen).

** Why CARBON and not generated audio (assessment, 2026-11-23)
- Output is symbolic and editable: notes, steps, scenes - not a rendered
  audio file. Every AI change can be inspected, undone, tweaked by hand.
- The human stays the performer: the LLM edits the backbone; the player
  keeps mutes, scenes, CCi, Eurorack spice. Matches the project direction.
- Real-time, tight timing comes from dedicated hardware; the LLM is never
  in the timing path, so its latency and failures cannot stop the music.
- It drives real instruments (MIDI, CV/gate, Eurorack): the sound stays the
  user's own, not a model's timbre.
- The constraints help: 6 tracks x 64 steps x 6 slots, scenes and bias give
  a small, well-defined action space - easier for an LLM to use reliably
  than open-ended composition, and easy to describe as a WoT TD.
- Novel and testable: voice-to-sequencer with bar-quantised, undoable edits
  is unusual; a WoT TD for a music device is a concrete standards test.
- Limits: the LLM's musical judgment is uneven; useful prompts need musical
  vocabulary; firmware SysEx work and the gateway are real effort; a
  conversational loop is slower than turning a knob for small changes.

** Scope and behaviour
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: scope-and-behaviour
:END:
Non-destructive, per-playback variation, not remembered evolution. Stored
notes remain unchanged. First scope: ordinary sequenced Notes, including drum
notes; live input, ordinary arp input/output and generated Chord stay
unchanged. Do not change existing ordinary arp input-duration/gate rules.

Each dimension has independent probability and amount; humanization defaults
Off and Off must preserve existing playback. Exact menu placement, ranges and
storage policy need a small design review before implementation.

- Velocity: signed variation around stored velocity (often 100), allowing both
  louder and softer attacks. Prefer small deviations over extremes. Clamp
  positive velocities to 1..127. Do not activate rests/zero-velocity events.
  Initial per-note decisions; correlated chord accents are later work.
- Gate length: symmetric percentage variation around normal effective duration
  after Track Gate scaling. Permit shorter and longer notes, minimum one tick.
  Duration begins at the actual delayed attack; onset delay must not silently
  shorten it. Define rounding and safe upper bounds.
- Late onset: add a fixed Centre delay plus probabilistic signed jitter to the
  existing scheduled note delay. Require Centre >= jitter amplitude so the
  extra delay is never negative. Unselected groups receive Centre only;
  selected groups receive Centre + jitter. Example Centre 3, jitter +/-2
  yields 1..5 extra ticks. Probability zero still retains Centre when enabled.
  Initially use one draw shared by the eligible note group of each track step,
  preserving existing per-note relative spacing; no unintended random strum.

** Timing and synchronisation
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: timing-and-synchronisation
:END:
Keep transport, clock output, step positions and control-event processing on
existing schedules. Delay audible Note attacks only. Schedule every offset
from the original onset, never cumulatively from a previous humanized attack.
This adds latency relative to external devices/unmodified tracks, not drift. A
common Centre across participating tracks gives a shared late reference, not
truly early notes relative to the original clock. No look-ahead or clock
redesign is intended. Resolution follows current internal tick scheduling.

Reuse existing delayed-note scheduling where practical. Draw decisions once
and retain them until playback; no redraw from GUI calls or scheduling
retries. Use dedicated random state, preferably per track, independent of
probability gates and other features. Define seeding/reset policy and
deterministic test hooks without requiring identical musical results on each
transport restart.

** Design checks before coding
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: design-checks-before-coding
:END:
- Specify controls: enable, velocity probability/amount, gate
  probability/amount, timing probability/Centre/jitter; exact distribution and
  bounds.
- Decide session-only versus saved per-track/per-scene settings explicitly.
  Review compatibility, snapshot/undo and host tooling before changing
  storage.
- Ratchets need an explicit policy: delayed whole train versus individual
  hits, gate scaling and release ownership. Do not silently break their
  current behaviour. A documented initial exclusion is preferable to ambiguous
  rules.
- Review interaction with conditional gates, explicit/inherited/relative
  delays, Hrm/output processing, Gli and note-slot limits. Humanization must
  not alter control-source resolution or conditional random draws.
- Changes to controls should affect newly scheduled events, not redraw already
  pending decisions. Follow existing safe cancellation boundaries and document
  any cases needing different handling.

** Verification and delivery
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: verification-and-delivery
:END:
- Host tests: Off equivalence; probability endpoints; signed velocity limits;
  gate rounding/minimum and duration from actual onset; Centre-only at zero
  probability; bounded jitter; shared timing with retained per-note delays.
- Integration tests: repeated pitches, overlaps, cross-step pending attacks,
  cancellation/Stop/scene/reset/mute cleanup, slot exhaustion, ratchet policy,
  no clock drift, random-stream independence, unchanged arp/Chord/live paths.
- Run all host suites, diff checks and forced release build. Run Rust tests if
  storage/tooling changes require them. Record identity, sizes, checksum and
  hardware gaps; no automatic flash, song write, publication or commit.
- Hardware audition should check velocity around 100, rhythmic feel at several
  tempos, gate lengths, external sync and busy-track note cleanup. Static
  tests are not realtime headroom or musical acceptance measurements.

** hum2 user revision - supersedes Centre/jitter above
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: hum2-user-revision---supersedes-centrejitter-above
:END:
User reports hum1 works but fixed Centre latency sounds poor. Replace Centre
and signed jitter with Late Chance 0..100% and Late Max 0..24 ticks.
Unselected groups receive zero extra delay; selected groups receive uniform
1..Max ticks. Max zero disables timing variation. Preserve shared group
spacing, independent velocity/gate and all prior exclusions. Session-only, no
migration. Arp feasibility reviewed in HUMANIZE.org; generated velocity is
local, but gate/ late onset need separate generated-output
lifetime/cancellation policy. No arp extension implemented. Wheel and
magnetism remain deferred.

** hum3 approved arp simplification
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: hum3-approved-arp-simplification

gate variation. Keep original expiry/program release schedules; late attacks
